# Executive summary

## Decision

Olist should protect its strongest category-state growth engines, fix a small set of material execution gaps before adding demand, and focus logistics investigation on a concentrated route portfolio—especially SP → RJ.

## Five headline insights

### 1. Scale expanded rapidly, but quality guardrails weakened

Matched Jan–Aug delivered GMV rose from R$2.99M to R$7.22M (+141.13%) as orders rose 139.94%; AOV moved only 0.49%, so the symmetric decomposition assigns 99.40% of GMV change to order volume. At the same time, on-time delivery fell from 96.50% to 92.27% and low reviews rose from 10.59% to 13.37%. Olist should manage growth and fulfillment quality as one allocation decision rather than celebrate scale alone.

### 2. Growth is concentrated enough to require deliberate protection

The top 10 categories explain 67.58% of GMV change, the top five customer states 76.03%, and São Paulo alone 43.56%. Thirteen high-scale, strong-execution category-state markets account for R$1.58M current GMV exposure. Protecting these engines with service and supply monitoring is more defensible than a marketplace-wide intervention.

### 3. Six markets should be fixed before more demand is added

Six top-quartile-value category-state markets contain 2,920 orders, R$409,170 current GMV exposure and 405 late orders while failing a peer execution guardrail. Four are Rio de Janeiro demand cells. Matching the eligible peer median would correspond arithmetically to 210 fewer late orders, but this is a benchmark gap—not a forecast or causal impact estimate.

### 4. Delivery risk is concentrated, and severe delays are closely associated with poor reviews

Thirteen high-late routes contain 1,439 late orders. SP → RJ alone contributes 653, or 45.38%, at a 15.72% late rate versus a 7.72% eligible-route median. Low-review incidence rises from 9.90% for near-on-time deliveries to 64.79% at 3–7 days late and 80.21% at 8+ days late. The association makes severe-delay prevention and recovery a customer-experience priority, while product and seller factors remain plausible alternatives.

### 5. Expansion and seller intervention can both be targeted

`stationery × SP` and `housewares × MG` are the two medium-confidence Grow candidates, together representing 1,019 orders and R$102,574 current GMV with strong execution. Separately, ten high-value weak sellers represent R$789,761 current GMV and 503 late orders. Olist should test incremental growth in the first portfolio while running seller/route-specific corrective diagnostics in the second.

## Recommended operating sequence

1. Freeze additional demand activation in the six Fix-before-growth cells while root causes are assigned.
2. Audit SP → RJ promise setting, mix and carrier execution; then inspect carrier-heavy SP → BA/CE/PA routes.
3. Monitor and protect the 13 Defend segments.
4. Run controlled growth tests in the two Grow segments with fulfillment guardrails.
5. Launch a ten-seller improvement portfolio using single-seller handling attribution and route/category controls.

All figures are observational and trace to numbered PostgreSQL scripts. Delivered GMV is a sales-value proxy, not Olist revenue or profit. No recommendation is an implemented outcome.
